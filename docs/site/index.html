Getting started with the Adaptive Learning Platform

Everything administrators, teachers, and students need to sign up, set up, and start learning — with step-by-step guides for each role.

What is AI4Talent?

AI4Talent is a class-based quiz platform for science education. Teachers create classes from their official roster, assign quizzes from a shared quiz pool, and invite students with a secure link. Students take quizzes online and, after each attempt, receive AI-generated feedback: a performance summary, targeted study recommendations pointing at specific pages of the class's learning materials, and interactive simulations that let them explore the concepts they struggled with.

Results are intentionally “blind” — students see their overall score and personalized guidance, but never the individual questions' correct answers, so quizzes stay reusable and the focus stays on learning the concepts.

Pick your role below to see your guide. Your school hosts its own AI4Talent server — all paths in this guide (like /login) are relative to that address.

Administrators run the platform: they curate the shared quiz pool, configure the AI features, manage every account, and keep the system healthy. Here's how to get set up and what each part of the admin dashboard does.

Setup Create your admin account

  1. Get the admin registration code.

    Admin sign-up is protected by a registration code configured on the server. Get it from whoever operates your AI4Talent deployment.

  2. Open /admin-register.

    Fill in your first name, last name, a username, your email, and a password, then enter the admin registration code and submit.

  3. Sign in at /login.

    You can sign in with either your email or your username. You'll land on the admin overview with stat cards for users, quizzes, and more.

Content Build the quiz pool

The quiz pool is the platform's shared library. Quizzes are grouped under topics; teachers browse this pool when adding quizzes to their classes.

  1. Create topics.

    On Quiz Pool, add topics (e.g. “Mechanics”, “Thermodynamics”) to organize the library.

  2. Add quizzes.

    Create a quiz under a topic, then open it to add questions in the quiz editor. Questions support single-select, multi-select (“select all that apply”), and numeric answers, with optional figures and image answer choices.

  3. Or import from PDFs.

    Use the Upload PDF Quizzes panel to turn existing PDF quizzes into editable quizzes automatically — see the next guide.

Content Import quizzes from PDFs

The Upload PDF Quizzes panel on the Quiz Pool page converts a batch of PDF files into pool quizzes using AI extraction. Each PDF becomes one quiz, named after the file.

  1. Pick a topic.

    Choose the destination topic from the dropdown. The file picker stays disabled until a topic is selected.

  2. Choose your PDFs.

    Select one or more PDF files, up to 20 pages each. Files whose names match a quiz that already exists under that topic are automatically skipped, so you can safely re-run a folder without creating duplicates.

  3. Watch the batch process.

    Files are handled one at a time, each moving through queued → creating → uploading → extracting. Extraction runs on the server, so it keeps going even while later files upload.

  4. Review and commit.

    When a file reaches ready (“N questions extracted — review and commit”), click Review to open the quiz editor, check the extracted questions and figures, fix anything the AI misread, and commit them. Extracted questions are never published without your review.

!

The footer tally (“X ready for review · Y skipped · Z failed…”) tracks the whole batch. If a file fails mid-flight, its partial quiz is rolled back automatically — fix the issue and just upload it again.

✓

PDF extraction requires an AI model assigned to the Quiz PDF Extraction use case — see AI configuration.

Content Generate simulations

Simulations are small interactive explorations (e.g. a projectile-motion sandbox) shown to students after a quiz to teach the concepts behind questions they missed — without revealing the questions' answers.

  1. Open Simulations and browse pool quizzes down to individual questions.
  2. Generate.

    Click generate on a question; a background worker builds a self-contained interactive simulation for its concept.

  3. Preview and curate.

    Preview the result, regenerate if it misses the mark (or restart one that got stuck), and delete any you don't want students to see.

✓

Generation requires a model assigned to the Question Simulation Generation use case and a running background worker.

People Manage users

The Users page lists every account, grouped into Admins, Teachers, and Students, with search across the lot.

  1. Onboard teachers.

    Teachers self-register at /register using the teacher registration code configured on the server — share it only with your teaching staff. Students never register directly; they join through class invite links.

  2. Remove accounts carefully.

    Deleting a user opens a confirmation explaining the consequences: removing a teacher also removes their classes and materials; removing a student removes their enrollments and progress. You cannot delete your own account.

System Configure the AI

On AI Config you connect model providers and decide which model powers each feature.

  1. Add a provider.

    Enter the provider type, base URL, and API key for any OpenAI-compatible endpoint.

  2. Discover models.

    Use model discovery to pull the provider's available models, and adjust their settings as needed.

  3. Assign models to use cases.

    Each AI feature runs on the provider + model you assign it:

    Use casePowers
    Teacher & Admin ChatThe assistant available to staff
    Student ChatThe student-facing assistant
    PDF Description GenerationPage summaries of learning materials (drives study recommendations)
    Quiz PDF ExtractionThe PDF quiz batch importer
    Question Simulation GenerationInteractive simulation building
!

A feature whose use case has no assignment will fail with a “no provider” error — assign every use case you intend to use, and check usage metrics on this page to monitor consumption.

System Email, backups & logs

Email (SMTP)

On Email, enter your SMTP host, port, username, password, from-address, and from-name, then use send test email to verify delivery. You can also cap how many emails each teacher may send.

Database backup

On Backup, run on-demand backups, list existing ones, test the storage connection (WebDAV / OneDrive / S3), and restore when needed.

System logs

On Logs, audit everything the platform records — logins, API activity, worker jobs, system events. Filter by category (AUTH, API, WORKER, USAGE, SYSTEM) and severity (info / warning / error), or free-text search by message, type, or IP.

Teachers create classes from a roster, invite students with a secure link, assign quizzes from the shared pool, upload learning materials that power AI study recommendations, and export grades straight back to the eLC gradebook.

Setup Create your teacher account

  1. Get the teacher registration code from your platform administrator.
  2. Open /register.

    Fill in your first name, last name, a username, your email, and a password, then enter the registration code and submit.

  3. Sign in at /login with your email or username. Your sidebar shows Dashboard, My Classes, Stats, Quizzes, and Messages.

Classes Create a class & upload the roster

A class is created together with its official student roster. The roster is what later verifies students' identities when they join, so it comes first.

  1. Export your classlist from eLC.

    In eLC (Brightspace), export your classlist as CSV. AI4Talent reads the standard export directly — it needs these columns (extra columns are ignored):

    ColumnAccepted headers
    Student IDOrgDefinedId · Id · Student ID · 81 Number (a leading # is fine)
    Last NameLast Name
    First NameFirst Name
    EmailEmail

    If the file has no header row, columns are read in the order ID, Last Name, First Name, Email. Rows missing an ID, name, or email are skipped.

  2. Go to My Classes → Create New Class.

    Enter the Class Name (e.g. “CSCI4300 Web Programming – Spring 2026”) and an optional description.

  3. Upload the roster CSV.

    Click the Class List (CSV) upload area and pick your file. A live preview table shows every parsed student (last name, first name, email) so you can confirm the import looks right.

  4. Click Create Class.

    You'll land on the class page, with cards for Quizzes, Learning Materials, and your roster.

!

Only students on the roster can join the class — the invite flow checks each student's 81 number against this list. If enrollment changes, update the class list before sharing the invite link with new students.

Classes Invite students

Students join through a tokenized invite link plus an identity check against your roster — no accounts to create by hand.

  1. Open the class and click Invite Link.
  2. Generate a link.

    Under Generate New Link, optionally set Expires in (days) and Max uses — leave them blank for a link that never expires and has unlimited uses. Click Generate.

  3. Copy and share.

    The link appears under Active Links with a Copy button, a usage counter, and its expiry. Share it with your class by email or your LMS.

  4. Students verify and join.

    Each student opens the link, enters their 81 number, and the platform matches it to your roster. Their name is pre-filled from the roster; they pick a username, email, and password, and are enrolled the moment the account is created. An 81 number can only register once.

✓

Set an expiry or max-uses on links you post somewhere semi-public. You can keep several active links with different limits at the same time.

Content Add, schedule & publish quizzes

Classes are assigned quizzes from the shared quiz pool. A quiz only becomes visible to students when you publish it.

  1. Open the class → Manage Quizzes.

    The page shows Assigned Quizzes (with Published/Draft badges) and an Add Quizzes section.

  2. Add quizzes from the pool.

    Pick quizzes from the pool, organized by topic. Need something custom? Create your own quizzes under Quizzes in the sidebar — they appear in your pool to assign.

  3. Configure quiz settings.

    Each assigned quiz has a settings dialog with:

    • Opens / Closes — the availability window; outside it students can't start the quiz.
    • Max attempts — cap the number of attempts, or leave blank for unlimited retries.
  4. Publish.

    Toggle Publish when the quiz is ready. You can unpublish or remove a quiz from the class at any time.

Content Upload learning materials

Learning materials (lecture notes, textbook chapters as PDFs) do double duty: students can study them, and the AI uses them to build page-level study recommendations after each quiz.

  1. Open the class → Manage Materials and upload your PDFs.
  2. Let processing finish.

    A background worker analyzes each document page-by-page. Once processed, post-quiz recommendations can point students at the exact pages that cover what they missed.

✓

Upload materials before students start taking quizzes — recommendations can only cite documents that exist in the class.

Results Monitor results & message students

  1. Class statistics.

    Open Statistics on the class page (or Stats in the sidebar for everything at once) for performance across quizzes.

  2. Per-quiz stats.

    Each assigned quiz has its own stats page showing how the class did on it.

  3. Per-student drill-down.

    Click a student in the roster to see their progress and open any individual attempt.

  4. Message students.

    Use Messages to send announcements; students see them under Notifications, and they can contact you from their class page.

Results Export grades to eLC

Quiz grades export as a CSV in the exact eLC (Brightspace) gradebook format, ready to import into the Grades tool with no manual editing.

  1. Open the quiz's stats page and click Export grades (eLC CSV).
  2. Name the grade column.

    The dialog pre-fills the eLC-style column header (e.g. Quiz 3 Points Grade <Numeric MaxPoints:100>); adjust it to match the gradebook item you're importing into.

  3. Download and import.

    The CSV contains every roster student with their best completed score (0–100). Import it in eLC's Grades tool.

!

Students who haven't completed the quiz appear without a score, so you can spot missing submissions during import.

As a student you join your class with an invite link from your teacher, take quizzes online, and get personalized AI feedback after every attempt — a performance summary, reading recommendations, and interactive simulations to explore what you missed.

Setup Join your class

There's no open sign-up page for students — your account is created the moment you join your first class through your teacher's invite link.

  1. Open the invite link your teacher shared. You'll see the class name and teacher so you know you're in the right place.
  2. Verify your identity.

    Enter your 81 number (your university ID — with or without the leading #). The platform checks it against the official class roster and shows your name once verified.

    • “81 not found” means you're not on the roster yet — ask your teacher to update the class list.
    • “Already registered” means an account already exists for your 81 number — just sign in instead.
  3. Create your account.

    Your name is pre-filled from the roster. Choose a username, enter your email, set a password, and click Create account & join class. You're signed in automatically and taken straight to your class.

✓

Already have an account from another class? Open the new invite link while signed in and you'll get a one-click Join button — no second account needed.

Setup Sign in & find your way around

  1. Sign in at /login with your email or username, plus your password.
  2. Your dashboard shows your enrolled classes, how many quizzes you've completed, and recent notifications. The sidebar has Dashboard, My Classes, Notifications, and Exam History.
  3. Open a class to see its quizzes grouped by topic. Each quiz shows Start, Continue (you have an attempt in progress), or Retry (you've completed it); quizzes outside their availability window appear locked.

Quizzes Take a quiz

  1. Click Start.

    An attempt begins immediately and the first question appears, with a progress bar and numbered dots for every question — answered ones turn green.

  2. Answer each question.
    • Single choice — pick one option.
    • “Select all that apply” — check every correct option.
    • Numeric — type a number (the unit is shown next to the field).
    • Click any figure or image choice to enlarge it.
  3. Move around freely.

    Use Previous/Next or jump straight to a question via its dot. Your progress is saved — if you leave, the quiz shows Continue next time.

  4. Submit.

    The Submit Quiz button unlocks once every question is answered. Watch the closing date, and note your teacher may cap attempts.

Feedback Understand your results

Right after you submit you'll see your score — and within moments, personalized feedback generated for your attempt:

  1. AI performance summary.

    A short readout of how you did and which concept areas need attention.

  2. Study recommendations.

    Up to three pointers into your class's learning materials, down to the specific page ranges that cover what you missed.

  3. Interactive simulations.

    Hands-on concept explorations matched to the questions you saw — open one and experiment until the idea clicks.

!

You won't see per-question correct answers — that's by design. The feedback tells you what to study, and if retries are allowed you can hit Retry Quiz once you've reviewed.

✓

Feedback keeps generating even if you close the page — find it later under Exam History, which stores the results of every attempt.

Progress Track progress & get help

  1. Exam History lists all your past attempts — per class or across everything — with their saved results pages.
  2. Notifications collects announcements and messages from your teachers (the sidebar badge shows unread count).
  3. Contact your teacher straight from the class page via the Contact teacher button.
