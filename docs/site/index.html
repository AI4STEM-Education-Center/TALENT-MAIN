Getting started with AI4Talent

A walkthrough of the Adaptive Learning Platform from both sides of a class: the teacher who runs it, and the student who takes the quizzes. Pick your role below — every screen links straight into the live site.

What is AI4Talent?

AI4Talent is a class-based quiz platform for science education. Teachers create classes from their official roster, import quizzes from a shared global pool (or build them from a PDF, which the AI reads for you), and invite students with a secure link. Students take quizzes online and, after each attempt, receive AI-generated feedback: a performance summary, targeted study recommendations pointing at specific pages of the class's learning materials, and interactive simulations that let them explore the concepts they struggled with.

Results are intentionally “blind” — students see their overall score, the questions they missed, their own submitted responses, and personalized guidance, but never the correct answers or unselected choices. Quizzes stay reusable while students still know what to revisit.

This guide is written against the deployment at dev.ai4talent.org. Every path in it (like /login) is a live link — click it to open that screen directly. If your institution runs its own instance, the paths are the same under your own domain.

How a class comes together dev.ai4talent.org

Each step picks up where the previous one leaves off. This is the order things happen in when a class goes live:

  • ✓The platform administrator prepares the deployment. They connect an AI provider, assign a model to each AI feature, and stock the shared global quiz pool. This is done for you before you start — if something AI-powered reports “no provider”, they're who to ask.
  • ✓A teacher registers and creates a class. Sign-up uses a teacher registration code from the administrator; the class is created together with its official roster — see Create a class.
  • ✓The teacher uploads learning materials. Each document gets an AI-generated summary and key-concept tags, and every page carries an AI-written key concept and description with the important pages labeled — see Learning materials.
  • ✓The teacher adds quizzes and publishes them. Import from the global pool with one click, build one from scratch, or turn an existing quiz PDF into a live quiz — see Import & publish a quiz and Import a quiz from a PDF.
  • ✓Students join with an invite link and take the quizzes. There is no open student sign-up: an account is created when a student joins their first class and verifies their identity against the roster — see How students join.
  • ✓Everyone reads what comes back. Students get AI feedback, study recommendations and simulations after each attempt; teachers get per-class, per-quiz and per-student statistics plus a gradebook export — see Monitor results.

A 15-minute tour

If the platform is already configured and you just want to see the full loop once, this is the shortest path through it:

  1. Sign in as a teacher and open a class from My Classes — or create one from a roster CSV if there isn't one yet.

  2. Open a learning material and read what the AI wrote: a document summary, key concepts, and a key concept + description for every page, with the core pages labeled.

  3. Import a quiz from the global pool, add it to the class, and publish it.

  4. Switch to a student account enrolled in that class and go straight to taking the quiz and reading the AI feedback.

  5. Back as the teacher, check the results and export the grades.

  6. Then the part worth seeing: upload a quiz PDF of your own and watch the AI extract its questions, choices and figures for you to review.

As a teacher you own the class: you create it from your official roster, invite your students, decide what they're assessed on — importing quizzes from the global pool, writing your own, or letting the AI read an existing quiz PDF — and read what comes back, from AI-labeled learning materials to per-student results and a gradebook export.

Classes Create a class from your roster

A class is created together with its official student roster, because the roster is what verifies students' identities when they join.

  1. Export your classlist from your LMS.

    In D2L Brightspace (eLC) and most learning management systems you can export the classlist as CSV. AI4Talent reads the standard export directly — it needs these columns (extra columns are ignored):

    ColumnAccepted headers
    Student IDOrgDefinedId · Id · Student ID · 81 Number (a leading # is fine)
    Last NameLast Name
    First NameFirst Name
    EmailEmail

    If the file has no header row, columns are read in the order ID, Last Name, First Name, Email. Rows missing an ID, name, or email are skipped.

  2. Go to My Classes → Create New Class.

    Enter the Class Name (e.g. “CSCI4300 Web Programming – Spring 2026”) and an optional description.

  3. Upload the roster CSV.

    Click the Class List (CSV) upload area and pick your file. A live preview table shows every parsed student (last name, first name, email) so you can confirm the import looks right.

  4. Click Create Class.

    You'll land on the class page, with cards for Quizzes, Learning Materials, and your roster.

!

Only students on the roster can join a class — the invite flow checks each student's ID against this list. If enrollment changes, update the class list before sharing the invite link with new students.

People Invite your students

Students join through a tokenized invite link plus an identity check against your roster; there are no accounts to create by hand.

  1. Open the class and click Invite Link.
  2. Generate a link.

    Under Generate New Link, optionally set Expires in (days) and Max uses — leave them blank for a link that never expires and has unlimited uses. Click Generate.

  3. Copy and share.

    The link appears under Active Links with a Copy button, a usage counter, and its expiry. Share it with your class by email or your LMS.

  4. Students verify and join.

    Each student opens the link and enters their student ID — the ID column from your roster, labeled 81 Number in the app — and the platform matches it to your roster. Their name is pre-filled from the roster; they pick a username, email, and password, and are enrolled the moment the account is created. Each ID can only register once.

✓

Set an expiry or max-uses on links you post somewhere semi-public. You can keep several active links with different limits at the same time.

Content Import a quiz from the global pool, then publish it

The platform ships with a global quiz pool curated by administrators. You import from it into your own quiz library, assign the copy to your class, and publish — a quiz is invisible to students until that last step.

  1. Open Quizzes in the sidebar and switch to the Global Pool tab.

    Pool quizzes are grouped by topic, each showing its question count. Click a quiz name to preview its questions read-only before you commit to it.

  2. Click Import.

    You get your own independent, editable copy under My Quizzes — edit, reword or delete questions freely without touching the shared pool. Already-imported quizzes are badged Imported, and Import again gives you a second copy if you want to vary it.

  3. Assign the quiz to your class.

    Go to your class → Manage Quizzes. The page lists Assigned Quizzes (with Published/Draft badges) and, below it, Add Quizzes — everything in your own library that isn't assigned yet. Click Add.

  4. Configure quiz settings.

    Each assigned quiz has a settings dialog with:

    • Opens / Closes — the availability window; outside it students can't start the quiz.
    • Max attempts — cap the number of attempts, or leave blank for unlimited retries.
  5. Publish.

    Toggle Publish when the quiz is ready. You can unpublish or remove a quiz from the class at any time.

✓

You can also build a quiz from scratch — Create New Quiz on the same page, with optional topic labels for grouping — or let the AI build one for you from a PDF, described next.

Highlight Turn any quiz PDF into a live quiz

Any quiz you already have as a PDF can become a quiz on the platform. Upload the file and the AI reads it — question text, answer choices, numeric answers, figures and the answer key — and hands you the result to check before a single question goes live.

  1. Open one of your own quizzes and find Import from PDF.

    Create or import a quiz first (a pool quiz you haven't imported is read-only, so it has no import card), open it from Quizzes, and click Choose PDF. Up to 20 pages per file.

  2. The pages are rendered, then extracted.

    Your browser turns each page into an image, uploads it, and the AI extracts the questions server-side — so extraction keeps running even if you navigate away. Come back to the quiz and the in-progress import is picked up where it left off.

  3. Review what the AI found.

    Every extracted question appears as an editable card next to the page it came from, so you can compare against the original:

    • Fix any misread wording, choice, or numeric answer and tolerance.
    • Figures come across too — a draggable crop box sits on the page image; nudge it to frame the figure exactly, or add a box to a figure the extractor missed. Image answer choices work the same way.
    • Delete questions you don't want.
    • If the PDF had no answer key, a banner tells you so and you set the correct answer for each question yourself.
  4. Commit.

    The button reads Commit N questions and stays locked while anything is incomplete (it tells you how many) — so a half-read question can never reach your students. Committed questions land in the quiz, ready to assign and publish like any other.

!

If extraction fails or an upload is interrupted, the card offers Retry or Discard — discarding removes the uploaded PDF and any review progress, leaving the quiz untouched. Nothing is ever added without your commit.

✓

Already have question banks in QTI format? The same page has Import from QTI ZIP — the ZIP is opened in your browser and only the parsed questions are sent to the server.

Content Learning materials — uploaded, described & labeled by the AI

Learning materials (lecture notes, textbook chapters as PDFs) do double duty: they're the class's reading, and they're what the AI cites when it tells a student which pages to study. Once a document is uploaded, the AI reads every page of it.

  1. Open your class → Manage Materials and upload your PDFs.

    Each document is listed with its page count and processing state; a background worker handles it page by page and the page picks up the result automatically once it's finished. You can also use Import Materials to pull documents you've already uploaded to another one of your classes.

  2. Click a document to see what the AI wrote.

    The document page opens onto its AI analysis:

    • Document Summary — an AI-written overview of the whole PDF, in an editable box. Reword it and click Save summary; students see your wording.
    • Key Concepts — the concepts the AI found across the document, shown as tags.
    • All Pages — every page as a thumbnail you can enlarge, each with an AI-written key concept and description of what a student should take from that page.
    • Core content — the tick that labels a page as one that matters. The header tells you how many pages are marked (“N marked as core content”), and study recommendations are built from those pages, so unticking filler keeps recommendations pointed at real teaching pages.
  3. Edit anything the AI got wrong.

    Key concept, description and the core-content tick are all editable per page and saved individually — your edits, not the AI's guess, are what drive students' recommendations from then on.

✓

Upload materials before students start taking quizzes — recommendations can only cite documents that exist in the class, so getting them in early means the first quiz attempt already produces page-level recommendations.

!

Students never wade through the whole PDF: after a quiz they get the specific page range they need, which opens as a full-size reader (see Understand your results).

Results Monitor results & message students

  1. Class statistics.

    Open Statistics on the class page, or Stats in the sidebar to see every class at once.

  2. Per-quiz stats.

    Each assigned quiz has its own stats page showing how the class did on it.

  3. Per-student drill-down.

    Click a student in the roster to see their progress and open any individual attempt.

  4. Message students.

    Use Messages to send announcements. Write a subject and a message — every student in the class gets it under Notifications, and anyone with an email address on file is automatically emailed a short notice with a link that opens it. The message itself always lives on the platform, never in the email. Those notices are queued and retried until they go out, so the message history shows how many are still queued, delivered, or failed. Students can contact you back from their class page.

Results Export grades to your gradebook

Quiz grades export as a CSV in the D2L Brightspace (eLC) gradebook format, ready to import into your LMS's Grades tool with no manual editing.

  1. Open the quiz's stats page and click Export grades (eLC CSV).
  2. Name the grade column.

    Paste the complete gradebook column header (e.g. Quiz 3 Points Grade <Numeric MaxPoints:100>). The dialog detects its maximum points automatically, and you can adjust the grade calculation or point value before exporting.

  3. Download and import.

    The CSV contains every roster student with their best completed score (0–100). Import it in your LMS's Grades tool.

!

Students who haven't completed the quiz appear without a score, so you can spot missing submissions during import.

As a student you take quizzes online and get personalized AI feedback after every attempt — a performance summary, reading recommendations that open the exact pages you need, and interactive simulations to explore what you missed.

Setup Join your class

There's no open sign-up page for students: your account is created the moment you join your first class through your teacher's invite link.

  1. Open the invite link your teacher shared. The class name and teacher are shown, so there's no doubt it's the right place.
  2. Verify your identity.

    Enter your student ID — the institution ID your teacher's roster is built from, labeled 81 Number in the app (with or without a leading #). The platform checks it against the official class roster and shows your name once verified.

    • “Not found” means you're not on the roster yet — ask your teacher to update the class list.
    • “Already registered” means an account already exists for that ID — sign in instead.
  3. Create the account.

    Your name is pre-filled from the roster. Choose a username, enter an email, set a password, and click Create account & join class — you're signed in automatically and taken straight to the class.

✓

Already have an account from another class? Open the new invite link while signed in and you get a one-click Join button — no second account needed.

Setup Sign in & find your way around

  1. Sign in at /login with your email or username, plus your password.
  2. Your dashboard at /student shows your enrolled classes, how many quizzes you've completed, and recent notifications. The sidebar has Dashboard, My Classes, Notifications, and Exam History.
  3. Open a class to see its quizzes grouped by topic. Each quiz shows Start, Continue (you have an attempt in progress), or Retry (you've completed it); quizzes outside their availability window appear locked.

Quizzes Take a quiz

  1. Click Start.

    An attempt begins immediately and the first question appears, with a progress bar and numbered dots for every question — answered ones turn green.

  2. Answer each question.
    • Single choice — pick one option.
    • “Select all that apply” — check every correct option.
    • Numeric — type a number (the unit is shown next to the field).
    • Click any figure or image choice to enlarge it.
  3. Move around freely.

    Use Previous/Next or jump straight to a question via its dot. Your progress is saved — if you leave, the quiz shows Continue next time.

  4. Submit.

    The Submit Quiz button unlocks once every question is answered. Watch the closing date, and note your teacher may cap attempts.

Feedback Understand your results

Right after you submit you'll see your score — and within moments, personalized feedback generated for your attempt:

  1. Questions to review.

    Each question you missed is shown with the response you submitted, including numeric values and image choices. Correct answers and choices you did not select stay hidden.

  2. AI performance summary.

    A short readout of how you did and which concept areas need attention.

  3. Study recommendations.

    Up to three pointers into your class's learning materials, down to the specific page ranges that cover what you missed — each with a short reason why it was picked for you. Click one and the pages open in a full-size reader you can scroll through, so you read exactly the pages that matter rather than hunting through the whole PDF.

  4. Interactive simulations.

    Hands-on concept explorations matched to the questions you saw — open one and experiment until the idea clicks.

!

You won't see per-question correct answers or unselected choices — that's by design. Review what you submitted, follow the study guidance, and if retries are allowed hit Retry Quiz once you're ready.

✓

Feedback keeps generating even if you close the page — find the missed-question review and AI guidance later under Exam History, which stores the results of every attempt.

Progress Track progress & get help

  1. Exam History lists all your past attempts — per class or across everything — with their saved results pages.
  2. Notifications collects announcements and messages from your teachers (the sidebar badge shows unread count). When a new one arrives you get an email at the address on your account telling you it's waiting, with a link that opens it here.
  3. Contact your teacher straight from the class page via the Contact teacher button.
